## About me

In real life I'm a fairly introverted person. Online, maybe a bit more so — but I really want to chat easily with everyone, even though a single sentence from someone can get me overthinking, and I often end up regretting things...

My hobbies? I don't seem to have any real hobbies. Thinking about what my hobbies actually are makes me despair. Running a server? Coding? Playing games? Is that really what I want...

Should I say I'm insecure? If I say I'm insecure, will people just peg me as a Jiahao — one of those posers doing the sad-boy bit? A joke? With only this much to offer, shouldn't I be insecure? I might be the one my age who believes most in the line "insecurity makes you better." But no amount of insecurity has ever been enough to overcome the laziness in my body? Am I lazy? I don't seem especially lazy, but there's a lot I simply haven't done — which is to say, I haven't stepped out of my comfort zone. How do I step out of my comfort zone? Oh — avoiding problems is a flaw of mine. Maybe sometimes it turns into a strength, but don't use it here, come on!

I don't seem to be very kind. Curled up in my own comfort zone, I've hurt quite a few other people... especially my family... and yet it seems I did nothing wrong. If I hadn't pushed back then, I certainly wouldn't be here talking with everyone. But there's no denying I hurt my family, and my family loves me. Maybe I have to break out of the cage to breathe? Or maybe I'm just too stupid, too lazy, and haven't achieved much academically? Maybe so. Really?

I seem to be reflecting. I seem to be turning toward good...... God... what am I saying?... I should be coherent, right? I should, right?

I'm in grade 10 now, and I miss grade 9, miss those busy days. It seems having someone keep an eye on you isn't so bad after all — maybe it's just the unfamiliarity of freedom and an unfamiliar environment that frightens me.

## My first repo

In the second half of grade 9, being able to get to a computer after tutoring — what a fine thing that was. I still remember: one of my tutors — a university student — saw that I was pretty good with AI and wanted to learn a thing or two from me. Late in the evening, just as the tutor was about to leave, the tutor and I sat in front of the computer together and opened the OpenAI website — and then got jumpscared by a creepy image...

During the day I'd plan what to do at night and write down whatever good ideas I'd had.

My first repo was made around then, for a friend in a group chat — or maybe I was just inspired by him? He's my age and was struggling to memorise vocabulary every day, and I suddenly thought: could I make a plugin for a Minecraft server that forces you to memorise words? (It doesn't force you any more, of course.) That became [Let-s-learn-English](https://github.com/sqkl520/Minecraft-server-plugin-Let-s-learn-English). It has a really comfortable chest GUI, which is easy on the eyes if you're as fussy about this as I am. I did bring it up in the group back then, but hardly anyone paid attention. It's hard to imagine that a small idea turned into something real. Two stars now — whether or not anyone else cares, whether or not anyone in real life cares, I'm proud of it.

Sadly, I didn't write a single line — it's just some vibe-coded thing. And that seems to make me insecure again.

It's sad: human self-worth got devalued right at this moment. Before this, turning an idea into a real thing used to take a long time and a lot of effort; now it's a matter of one sentence — and that in turn drags money into it, raising the bar for ordinary people. Any day now, someone else might beat me to my idea, and that probability only grows. What value do we still have? When everything becomes utilitarian, everything feels meaningless...

But creating meaning often happens in the process: during the day I write down an idea, at night I dig into it. That "what a fine thing" evening is valuable in itself. It isn't a means to an end; it's part of my life. I know that sounds like chicken soup for the soul, but it seems to be the best way we ordinary people have of pushing back against the AI wave — and against the universe's eventual doom as a settled fact. Maybe the process really is what matters most?

Enjoy the process of making things — what if it works out? Maybe the result really doesn't matter...
